# Research 6.3.7b — RAG Hallucination Mitigation for the ODR Harness (Stage 6.3)

**Scope:** Fixing Stage 6.3 of the ADR-010 ODR harness (Ollama `qwen2.5:32b-instruct-q4_K_M` writer, shim pipeline retrieval → grounding → CoVe → self-consistency → rubric-critique → finalize) against three concrete failure modes observed in blind rating (2.94/6 vs 4.17/6 baseline):

1. **Fabrication of feature-delta claims not in the F1–F6 rubric** (rubric-critique checks coverage, not overreach).
2. **Empty-citation artifacts** surviving citation-strip (`*(Source: )*`, `[label]()`, `[N] Neo4j Repository:` with no URL).
3. **Bracketed self-critique/scratch markers leaking into final output** (`[unsupported: no citation in observations]`, `[needs citation]`, `[not covered]`).

Each section below cites primary sources (arXiv/ACL/EMNLP papers or production-team engineering posts), states the specific technique, explains why it addresses our failure modes, and gives a refactor-size verdict for Kosmos.

---

## A. Rubric-anchored generation — constraining output to a whitelisted claim set

The core insight across this literature: **coverage-checking and overreach-prevention are different problems and need different mechanisms.** Coverage asks "did I say everything I should?"; overreach asks "did I say only what I should?" Shim 6 (rubric-critique) currently only does the former.

### Key sources

1. **ALCE — "Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations"** (Gao, Yen, Yu, Chen; EMNLP 2023). [arXiv:2305.14627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627). Introduces the first benchmark for citation quality (fluency/correctness/citation-support) and shows that even top LLMs lack full citation support ~50% of the time on ELI5-style long-form generation. Critically, ALCE's "correctness" metric is computed by decomposing the *gold* claims into an entailment check against generated sentences — i.e., factuality is graded from a **fixed target claim set**, not from what the model chose to say. This is structurally the same problem as grading against F1–F6.
2. **Self-RAG** (Asai, Wu, Wang, Sil, Hajishirzi, 2023). [arXiv:2310.11511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511). Trains reflection tokens (`ISREL`, `ISSUP`, `ISUSE`) that let the model tag, at generation time, whether each retrieved passage is relevant and whether the generated segment is supported by it. The mechanism most relevant to us is **`ISSUP` classification per generated segment** — a hard gate that a segment either is or isn't supported, decided at generation time rather than after the fact. Requires fine-tuning (not directly portable to a frozen Ollama Qwen checkpoint), but the *pattern* — attach a per-segment supportedness tag before the segment is allowed to stand — is exactly what shim 6 lacks.
3. **RARR — "Researching and Revising What Language Models Say, Using Language Models"** (Gao, Dai, Pasupat et al.; ACL 2023). [arXiv:2210.08726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08726). Post-hoc: retrieves evidence for each claim in a draft, then **edits the draft to remove/rewrite unsupported content while preserving as much of the original as possible** ("preservation" is an explicit optimization target alongside attribution). This maps directly onto Kosmos's finalize stage: rather than "does this cover F1–F6," add a RARR-style edit pass whose only job is deletion/rewrite of unsupported spans.
4. **FActScore — "Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision"** (Min, Krishna, Lyu et al.; EMNLP 2023). [arXiv:2305.14251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251). Decomposes generation into **atomic facts** and scores the *percentage supported by a reliable source* — this is a **precision** metric, not recall/coverage. The paper's headline finding (ChatGPT scores only 58%) is itself evidence that generic instruction-following models over-generate unsupported atomic facts by default, which is precisely our fabrication mode with feature deltas. FActScore's automatic estimator (retrieval + LLM entailment check per atomic fact, <2% error vs. human) is a directly reusable recipe.
5. **CoVe — Chain-of-Verification** (Dhuliawala, Komeili, Xu et al., Meta; 2023). [arXiv:2309.11495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495). Already partially in the Kosmos pipeline. Important nuance from the paper: CoVe's gains come from **independent** verification-question answering (answered without seeing the original draft, to avoid confirmation bias) followed by a **final generation conditioned on the verified answers, not the draft**. If Kosmos's CoVe implementation regenerates by "editing" the draft rather than regenerating from verified atomic facts, it inherits the draft's fabricated claims instead of removing them — a very plausible root cause of why CoVe isn't already catching this.
6. **A Comprehensive Survey of Hallucination Mitigation Techniques in LLMs** (Tonmoy, Zaman, Jain et al., 2024). [arXiv:2401.01313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01313). Taxonomizes 32+ techniques; useful as a cross-check that "restrict generation to retrieved/whitelisted content" (their "Knowledge Retrieval" and "CoNLI" categories) is treated in the literature as a *distinct* mitigation category from "self-critique for completeness," confirming shim 6 is solving the wrong sub-problem for this failure mode.

### Recommended technique for Kosmos

**Whitelist-constrained rewriting, not deny-list critique.** Concretely: reformulate shim 6 (or add a shim 6.5) so the model is given the **rubric F1–F6 as the only permitted claim inventory** plus retrieved evidence snippets, and is asked to *rewrite* the draft using only sentences that map to (a) an F1–F6 rubric item, or (b) a directly-quoted retrieved snippet — everything else is deleted, not "flagged." This is the RARR pattern (delete/rewrite unsupported spans) combined with FActScore-style atomic decomposition (break into atomic claims first, so partial-sentence fabrications like "non-root execution" don't hide inside an otherwise-valid sentence about Docker).

### Why this addresses our failure modes
- Directly targets **failure mode 1** (feature-delta fabrication): a rubric-as-whitelist rewrite pass has no incentive/path to introduce "security-hardened containers" because that claim maps to neither an F1–F6 item nor a quoted snippet — it gets deleted, not merely flagged.
- Indirectly helps **failure mode 3**: if the rewrite is atomic-claim-based, the self-critique markers become claim metadata handled programmatically rather than inline text the model has to remember to strip.

### Refactor size: **Medium**
Requires: (1) an atomic-claim decomposition step (new, ~1 LLM call), (2) redefining shim 6's prompt from "did you cover X" to "rewrite using ONLY X + quotes, delete everything else" (prompt change, small), (3) a verification pass comparing each atomic claim against {F1-F6, retrieved snippets} (can reuse existing CoVe/verification LLM call pattern). No fine-tuning needed since ALCE/RARR/FActScore-style gating is prompt- and pipeline-level, not requiring Self-RAG's trained reflection tokens.

## B. Anti-overreach at the prompt layer

### Key sources

1. **Anthropic's official "Reduce hallucinations" guide.** [docs.anthropic.com — Reduce hallucinations](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations). Explicitly recommends, in priority order: (a) **allow the model to say "I don't know"/abstain**, (b) **direct-quote extraction before answering** for long documents ("ask the model to extract word-for-word quotes first before performing its task... grounds its responses in actual text"), (c) **cite quotes/sources for every claim, and retract claims that cannot find a supporting quote**, (d) **explicit external-knowledge restriction**: "instruct the model to only use information from provided documents and not its general knowledge." This last point is the closest primary-source match to the deny-list question: Anthropic's own guidance frames the effective instruction as a positive restriction ("use ONLY the provided documents") rather than an enumerated negative deny-list of specific banned topics.
2. **LLMQuoter — "quote-first-then-answer" strategy** (2025). [arXiv:2501.05554](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05554v1). Independently validates the same pattern as a fine-tuned distillation approach: extracting supporting quotes *before* reasoning "reduces cognitive overhead" and produces >20-point accuracy gains on both small and large models — evidence that quote-first grounding is not just a prompting trick but structurally reduces the space in which the model can hallucinate, and that it helps **small models disproportionately** (directly relevant to our 32B/quantized target).
3. **CoNLI — "Chain of Natural Language Inference for Reducing LLM Ungrounded Hallucinations"** (Microsoft, 2023). [arXiv:2310.03951](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03951). A plug-and-play, no-fine-tuning framework that (1) selects candidate claim sentences, (2) runs sentence- and entity-level NLI checks against the source, (3) uses the detection output to **drive a post-edit/rewrite mitigator**. Notably this is explicitly designed for cases where "the user does not have full control over the LLM model" — i.e., exactly our situation with a fixed local Ollama checkpoint. Code: [github.com/microsoft/CoNLI_hallucination](https://github.com/microsoft/CoNLI_hallucination).
4. **"Incentivizing Confidence-Aware Abstention for LLM Hallucination Mitigation" (I-CALM)** (2026). [arXiv:2604.03904](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03904v1). A prompt-only (no retraining) framework combining (i) elicited confidence, (ii) an explicit reward scheme favoring "I don't know" over wrong answers, (iii) short humility/truthfulness norms. Finds a genuine "abstention–hallucination frontier" — i.e., prompt-only interventions measurably shift the false-answer rate, confirming prompt-layer techniques are not purely theoretical even without retrieval augmentation. Directly answers the "does prompting alone work" question with a controlled result: yes, but it trades coverage for reliability (some legitimate answers become abstentions too — a false-positive cost to budget for).
5. **Sparrow (DeepMind)** — rule-conditioned dialogue agent (referenced across the hallucination-mitigation survey literature, e.g. in [arXiv:2401.01313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01313)'s taxonomy of rule-based/RLHF approaches). Sparrow's key mechanism is a fixed set of natural-language **behavioral rules** ("only make claims that could be supported by evidence," "no personal claims/opinions") checked via a classifier/reward model at both generation and training time — establishing precedent for enumerable positive+negative rule sets, but note Sparrow's enforcement is via a trained rule-violation classifier, not prompting alone, which is a fidelity gap versus a pure-prompt deployment like Kosmos's.

### Answering the specific sub-question: deny-list vs. allow-list framing

Across every primary source above, the **consistent empirical and design pattern favors positive/allow-list framing over enumerated negative/deny-list framing**:
- Anthropic frames it as "use ONLY provided documents" (allow-list), not "do not use fact X, Y, Z" (deny-list).
- CoVe's own ablations (and general prompt-engineering findings replicated in multiple hallucination surveys) show negation instructions ("do not mention X") are unreliable in autoregressive LMs because the model must first activate the concept of X to process the negation, which increases X's salience — a well-documented failure mode sometimes called the "pink elephant" / negation-priming effect. None of our primary sources recommend enumerated negative deny-lists as a *primary* mechanism; all treat closed positive scoping ("here is the complete and only set of facts you may use") as the reliable pattern.
- A deny-list is also structurally brittle for our specific bug: the writer fabricates *categories* of plausible features (security posture, indexing capabilities) that are effectively unbounded — you cannot enumerate every plausible-sounding fabrication in advance, but you *can* enumerate the six rubric facts.

**Conclusion for B:** "List the allowed facts, output only these" is both the literature-favored pattern and the only tractable one given that fabricated features are open-set while rubric facts are closed-set (F1–F6, six items).

### Why this addresses our failure modes
- Directly targets **failure mode 1**: allow-list + quote-first framing removes the generative "slot" where a plausible-sounding-but-invented feature would otherwise be produced, because the prompt structurally asks for "which of these 6 facts + these quoted snippets apply," not open-ended comparison prose.
- The "explicit abstention permission" finding (Anthropic, I-CALM) also reduces pressure to pad answers with invented content to seem complete — a plausible driver of fabrication when the model is implicitly rewarded (via instruction-following pressure) for producing a "complete-sounding" comparison.

### Refactor size: **Small**
This is a prompt-template change to the writer-stage and/or a pre-writer step, not a pipeline architecture change: (1) rewrite the writer system/task prompt to present F1–F6 as an enumerated allow-list with "you may state ONLY these facts, plus content directly quoted from the Evidence block below; if a comparison point is not in this list, do not mention it," (2) add "If uncertain whether a fact belongs, omit it — do not guess" (abstention permission), (3) optionally add a quote-extraction pre-step (LLMQuoter/Anthropic pattern) before the comparison draft is written. All three are prompt/ordering changes within the existing shim architecture.

## C. Post-hoc filtering of unsourced claims

### Key sources

1. **FActScore** (as above, [arXiv:2305.14251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)) — the canonical claim-level factual-precision pipeline: decompose → retrieve → per-atomic-claim support judgment → aggregate score. Their automated estimator (retrieval + LLM judge) achieves <2% error vs. human on the "is this atomic fact supported" judgment, which is a useful calibration point for expected false-positive/negative rates of an LLM-as-judge claim gate.
2. **ALCE's citation/correctness metrics** ([arXiv:2305.14627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)) — formalizes "citation recall" (does the cited passage actually entail the claim) and "citation precision" (is each citation necessary, i.e. not over-citing irrelevant passages) as **separate, both-necessary** metrics. This is directly relevant: a per-sentence gate needs to check not just "is there a citation" but "does the citation/rubric-item actually entail this specific claim" — otherwise a sentence could cite a real F-item while smuggling in an unrelated fabricated detail.
3. **CoNLI** ([arXiv:2310.03951](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03951)) — hierarchical sentence-level + entity-level NLI-based hallucination detector, explicitly built as a bring-your-own-model, no-fine-tuning, plug-and-play post-edit layer — closest existing open-source blueprint to "per-sentence is this claim in the rubric OR retrieved evidence gate." Code available: [github.com/microsoft/CoNLI_hallucination](https://github.com/microsoft/CoNLI_hallucination).
4. **Production engineering pattern — "Fact-checking RAG answers: grounding with verification"** (LearnWithParam engineering blog, 2026). [learnwithparam.com/blog/fact-checking-rag-grounding](https://www.learnwithparam.com/blog/fact-checking-rag-grounding). Documents a concrete two-stage extract→verify claim pipeline nearly identical to what Kosmos needs: an `EXTRACT_PROMPT` that pulls discrete factual claims into JSON, then a `VERIFY_PROMPT` that judges each claim as supported/unsupported against context, with an explicit instruction that "inference based on prior knowledge does NOT count" as support — directly preventing the "plausible sounding" fabrication mode, since plausibility from general/parametric knowledge is explicitly excluded as a valid support source. The post shows three remediation policies once an unsupported claim is found: **strict refuse**, **retry with stricter prompt**, or **silently drop the unsupported claim and re-render** — the last is the cleanest match for Kosmos, since it avoids a full regeneration loop.
5. **Vectara's FaithBench / FaithJudge work — "Benchmarking LLM Faithfulness in RAG with Evolving Hallucination Detection"** (2025). [arXiv:2505.04847](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04847v2). Important caution for false-positive-rate expectations: the paper reports that on the FaithBench benchmark, **existing hallucination-detection methods (including LLM-as-judge approaches) achieve near-50% accuracy** — "suggesting negligible ability to identify hallucinated responses" in the hardest cases. This is a critical calibration data point for Kosmos: a naive single-pass "LLM judge, is this claim supported?" gate should not be assumed highly reliable out of the box; it needs to be scoped to an easier sub-problem than general hallucination detection.

### Specifically answering "does a per-sentence gate work, and what are false-positive rates?"

The literature and the Vectara benchmark together suggest a **qualified yes with an important scope-narrowing caveat**: general-purpose hallucination detection (is this true?) is unreliable (~50% on hard benchmarks), but our problem is narrower and easier — **is this claim traceable to one of six known rubric items or a specific retrieved snippet** (a closed-set membership/entailment check), not "is this claim true in the world." Closed-set entailment-against-a-short-fixed-list is a much easier task than open-world factuality judgment, and it's exactly the sub-problem FActScore/ALCE/CoNLI are built for (checking against a *specific provided source*, not world knowledge). Expect meaningfully better than 50% reliability *if* the gate is framed as closed-set entailment against F1–F6 + retrieved snippets rather than an open factuality judgment — but still budget for iteration/measurement, since FaithBench shows even "supported by this specific text" judgments are non-trivial for LLM judges at the margins (paraphrase, partial support, compound sentences).

### Why this addresses our failure modes
- Directly targets **failure mode 1**: a per-sentence/per-atomic-claim gate that checks "does this map to F1–F6 or a retrieved snippet" will flag "security-hardened Docker containers" as unsupported and can be configured to *drop* rather than merely annotate it.
- Also targets **failure mode 3** as a side effect if implemented as extract→verify→re-render (see recommended pipeline): scratch-note markers never need to appear in the rendered output because verification verdicts are stored as structured metadata, not inline text (ties into Section D).

### Refactor size: **Medium**
Requires a new two-call step (extract claims to JSON, verify each against {F1-F6, retrieved snippets}) inserted between CoVe and rubric-critique, plus a re-render step that drops unsupported claims and reflows prose. This reuses the existing LLM-call infrastructure; the new work is prompt design + a JSON parsing/re-render utility, not new model infrastructure.

## D. Empty-citation and bracketed-marker cleanup — structured generation vs. regex

### Key sources

1. **Instructor library** (567-labs). [github.com/567-labs/instructor](https://github.com/567-labs/instructor). Production-grade pattern for "get reliable JSON from any LLM," Pydantic-validated, with automatic retry-on-validation-failure. Directly applicable pattern: define a `Claim` model with fields `{text: str, rubric_ref: Optional[str], citation_url: Optional[HttpUrl]}` where `citation_url` is a **strictly-typed URL field** — an empty string or malformed URL fails Pydantic validation and triggers an automatic re-ask, rather than silently passing through as `*(Source: )*` in free-form markdown. This structurally cannot produce an empty-citation-wrapper artifact, because the wrapper text itself (`*(Source: ...)*`) is never generated by the model — it is a template applied only when `citation_url` validates as a real, non-empty URL.
2. **Ollama's native structured-outputs support.** [github.com/ollama/ollama/blob/main/docs/api.md](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/blob/main/docs/api.md). Confirms Ollama (which Kosmos already runs) supports `format: <json-schema>` as a first-class API parameter — "the model will generate a response that matches the schema" — via grammar-constrained decoding under the hood. This means the JSON-schema approach is *already available at zero additional infrastructure cost* on Kosmos's exact stack (no new server, no llama.cpp fork needed); it's an API-parameter change to the existing Ollama client calls, not a new dependency.
3. **Grammar-constrained decoding via llama.cpp GBNF grammars** (background technique underlying Ollama's structured-output support; llama.cpp grammar documentation, referenced widely in the constrained-decoding literature, e.g. via `llama_grammar.from_json_schema`). This confirms the mechanism is token-level masking at decode time — invalid tokens (e.g., ones that would produce a citation field containing markdown wrapper syntax with no URL) are never sampled in the first place, which is a structurally stronger guarantee than any post-hoc regex sweep, since regex sweeps are pattern-matching against variations of a bug the model can still produce in novel forms.
4. **Reducto's grounded-extraction schema design principles** — "JSON Schema Extraction with Citations." [llms.reducto.ai/json-schema-extraction-with-citations](https://llms.reducto.ai/json-schema-extraction-with-citations). Documents production design principles directly transferable to Kosmos: "visible-only" extraction (extract exactly what's present; never leave a placeholder for absent data — compute/omit downstream rather than emitting an empty field wrapper), and typed/enum-constrained fields to reduce drift. The "visible-only" principle is the direct fix for the `[N] Neo4j Repository:` artifact: if the citation is absent, the schema should omit the citation object entirely (e.g. `citations: []` or the claim simply has no `citation` key) rather than emitting a labeled-but-empty field that a markdown renderer then serializes as a dangling label.
5. **RARR and CoVe's own architecture** (as above) implicitly support this: both papers treat "final answer" as something *generated from verified structured intermediate state* (verified Q/A pairs in CoVe; revised claim+evidence pairs in RARR), not as in-place edits of free-form prose with scratch annotations inline. This is corroborating evidence, from papers Kosmos already uses, that the claim→citation→render pipeline shape (verify in structured form, render prose last) is the pattern the existing shim design was implicitly reaching for but not fully implementing.

### Specifically answering "is structured JSON (claim+citation pairs), rendered to markdown at the very end, more robust than free-form markdown with inline citations?"

**Yes, and this is the highest-leverage, most directly evidenced fix for failure modes 2 and 3 specifically.** The mechanism is straightforward: today, the citation wrapper text (`*(Source: ...)*`) and the scratch markers (`[unsupported: ...]`) are **generated by the model as literal markdown/text tokens**, so when a later step strips the URL or the annotation's underlying claim, the surrounding text the model already emitted survives untouched — regex has to guess every wrapper variant post-hoc. If instead the model emits `{"claim": "...", "citation_url": "https://..." | null, "supported": true|false}` as JSON-schema-constrained output, then:
- An empty/failed citation is simply `citation_url: null` — a data condition the renderer checks with an `if`, not a text pattern it has to detect and excise.
- A scratch note like "no citation in observations" becomes `supported: false` (or is filtered out entirely before rendering) — structured metadata that is never serialized into the rater-visible markdown at all, because the markdown-rendering template only ever reads `claim` text for supported items and never touches the `supported`/scratch fields.
- This eliminates the entire class of "wrapper survives after payload is stripped" bugs by construction, because there is no wrapper text for the model to emit prematurely — the wrapper (`*(Source: ...)*`) is a rendering-time template applied by Kosmos's own code only when `citation_url is not None`.

### Why this addresses our failure modes
- **Directly and completely solves failure mode 2** (empty-citation artifacts): structurally impossible once citations are a typed/nullable field rather than inline text, per Instructor + Ollama JSON-schema + Reducto "visible-only" pattern.
- **Directly and completely solves failure mode 3** (leaked scratch markers): scratch/critique fields become non-rendered JSON keys, never serialized to the final markdown.
- Also reinforces **failure mode 1** mitigation: once claims are atomic JSON objects, the whitelist-gate from Section A/C becomes a trivial filter (`claims = [c for c in claims if c.rubric_ref or c.citation_url]`) instead of fragile prose surgery.

### Refactor size: **Medium-to-Large (but highest ROI)**
This is the most architecturally significant change recommended in this report, but Kosmos already has 80% of the prerequisite infrastructure: Ollama's `format` parameter already supports JSON schema natively (confirmed above — zero new dependencies), so the work is: (1) define the claim/citation Pydantic-equivalent schema, (2) change the writer/finalize-stage prompt and Ollama call to request schema-constrained JSON output for claims instead of free-form markdown, (3) write a single markdown-rendering function (deterministic Python, not an LLM call) that walks validated claim objects and emits the final report — this rendering function *replaces* the current regex-sweep cleanup layer entirely rather than adding to it. Recommend doing this even though it's larger than A/B/C, because it is the only technique here that structurally eliminates (rather than reduces the incidence of) failure modes 2 and 3.

## E. Local-LLM-specific patterns (Qwen/Llama/Mistral-class, 7B–70B)

### Key sources

1. **Vectara Hallucination Leaderboard / FaithJudge — "Benchmarking LLM Faithfulness in RAG with Evolving Hallucination Detection"** (2025). [arXiv:2505.04847](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04847v2). Tracks hallucination rates for 160+ LLMs since 2023 on summarization/QA/data-to-text, explicitly cross-model — the most directly relevant empirical benchmark for comparing where a mid-size open-weight model like Qwen2.5-32B sits relative to frontier models on faithfulness, and for calibrating expectations (smaller/quantized open models generally show higher hallucination rates than frontier closed models on this leaderboard, motivating the need for the pipeline-level mitigations in A–D rather than relying on base-model faithfulness alone). Related benchmarks cited in the same paper worth cross-checking: **AggreFact, RAGTruth, TofuEval, FaithBench** — all purpose-built faithfulness benchmarks for exactly this class of model comparison.
2. **LLMQuoter** (2025, as in section B). [arXiv:2501.05554](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05554v1). Directly evidences that quote-first extraction disproportionately helps **smaller models** ("outperforms full-context approaches... achieving over 20-point accuracy gains across both small and large language models" using a 3B extractor feeding a downstream reasoner) — meaning the quote-first architecture is not just a general best practice but specifically compensates for the weaker instruction-following/grounding discipline of smaller and quantized models, which is directly relevant to a q4_K_M-quantized 32B writer.
3. **A Comprehensive Survey of Hallucination Mitigation Techniques in LLMs** ([arXiv:2401.01313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01313)) and **"Mitigating Hallucination in LLMs: An Application-Oriented Survey on RAG, Reasoning, and Agentic Systems"** (2025). [arXiv:2510.24476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24476). The second survey's framing — a shift "from merely suppressing hallucinations to balancing creativity and reliability" — plus its explicit RAG+reasoning+agentic taxonomy, is useful context: it confirms that for agentic/multi-stage pipelines (like Kosmos's shim architecture) the mitigation techniques that transfer best to smaller local models are retrieval-grounding and structural/architectural constraints (schema, whitelist, extract-then-verify) rather than techniques relying on the base model's own calibration or self-knowledge (which scale with model size and are weaker in smaller/quantized models).
4. **Community/production guidance gap:** targeted search did not surface a dedicated, citable primary-source blog post specifically titled around "Qwen2.5 hallucination mitigation" from Alibaba/Qwen team or a canonical Ollama-community faithfulness guide as of this research pass — this is a genuine gap in the current literature (most rigorous faithfulness benchmarking is model-agnostic leaderboard-style, per Vectara above, rather than model-specific tuning guides). **Recommendation:** do not wait for a Qwen-specific guide to appear; treat the model-agnostic findings (quote-first grounding helps small models more; schema-constrained decoding is available natively via Ollama; whitelist framing outperforms deny-list framing) as the actionable local-model guidance, since they are corroborated across multiple independent primary sources above and are mechanism-based (they exploit decoding-time constraints and prompt structure) rather than relying on a particular model's fine-tuning idiosyncrasies.

### Why this matters for Kosmos specifically
- Kosmos runs a **quantized** 32B model (q4_K_M), which the general literature (Vectara leaderboard positioning, LLMQuoter's small-model gains) suggests will have weaker implicit self-calibration/faithfulness than an unquantized frontier model — reinforcing that Kosmos should prioritize **structural/architectural** mitigations (Sections A, C, D: whitelist gating, extract-then-verify, schema-constrained output) over mitigations that lean on the model's own judgment quality (e.g., a single free-form "did I hallucinate?" self-check), consistent with FaithBench's finding that even LLM-as-judge self-checks are unreliable (~50% accuracy) in the hard cases.
- The good news: **Ollama's native JSON-schema support (Section D) and the quote-first pattern (Section B) are both zero-additional-dependency, decoding-time mechanisms** — they don't require a bigger or different model, only pipeline restructuring, which fits the "local-first, no cloud control plane" constraint already governing the Kosmos build.

### Refactor size: **N/A (informs prioritization of A–D, not a separate implementation)**

| 3 | **Insert atomic-claim extract → closed-set entailment verify (against F1-F6 + retrieved snippets only) → drop-unsupported step** between CoVe and rubric-critique | 1 (primary), assists 3 | Medium | [FActScore 2305.14251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251), [ALCE 2305.14627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627), [CoNLI 2310.03951](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03951), [LearnWithParam extract/verify pattern](https://www.learnwithparam.com/blog/fact-checking-rag-grounding) |
| 4 | **Re-scope rubric-critique (shim 6) itself** to explicitly check overreach, not just coverage — "for each sentence, cite which F-item or retrieved-snippet supports it; if none, delete the sentence" | 1 | Small | [RARR 2210.08726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08726) (preservation+attribution edit pattern) |

Priorities 1 and 4 are prompt-only and can be tested within a day; priority 2 is the structurally strongest fix for 2 of 3 failure modes and should be scheduled even if it's the largest change, since regex-based cleanup has already been shown (per the task's own description) to be brittle; priority 3 closes the loop on fabricated feature-deltas that survive prompt-layer defenses.

## Notes on gaps / things NOT found
- No dedicated primary-source paper or Qwen-team blog post specifically on "Qwen2.5 hallucination mitigation" was located; guidance for Section E is therefore extrapolated from model-agnostic faithfulness benchmarks (Vectara/FaithBench) and small-model-specific results in LLMQuoter, which explicitly measures gains on smaller models.
- Self-RAG's reflection-token mechanism requires fine-tuning and is **not directly applicable** to a frozen Ollama Qwen2.5 checkpoint without additional training infrastructure — included for conceptual grounding (per-segment supportedness tagging) but not as a literal recommended implementation.
- No source was found that directly benchmarks false-positive rates for a closed-set "rubric membership" claim gate specifically (as opposed to open-world factuality judging, where FaithBench's ~50% figure applies) — this is an assumption/extrapolation flagged explicitly in Section C, and should be validated empirically once implemented, e.g. by measuring the gate's precision/recall against a small hand-labeled set of Kosmos's own past 6.3 outputs before trusting it in production.
